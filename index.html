Our story

It begins, as it always does, with mint tea.

Before anything else at Kassem's, there is tea — poured high from a silver pot until the glass froths, sweet with fresh mint. It is how guests have been welcomed in Moroccan homes for generations, and it is how we welcome you.

Kassem's Lounge is a family-run kitchen and lounge in Hemel Hempstead, just opposite the Water Gardens. The recipes come from home: harira the way it is ladled out at dusk, tagines left on a low flame until the lamb gives up arguing, couscous steamed and raked and steamed again, bread that exists chiefly to chase the sauce.

We cook the whole Moroccan day — breakfasts of msemen and amlou, long lunches, slow dinners — and we keep the pot warm for whoever comes through the door.

The lounge

Lantern light, low tables, no hurry.

A lounge, in the Moroccan sense: a place you settle into, not a table you're turned from. Come at dusk and stay past it.

The welcome

Tea arrives before the menu does. In Morocco hospitality isn't a service style, it's a reflex — and this is a family room as much as a dining room.

The food

Nothing here is rushed, because nothing in a tagine can be. Spices are toasted, broths are patient, and dishes arrive when they're ready — properly ready.

The pace

Linger. Order another pot of tea, split one more pastry, let the conversation go around again. That's what the cushions are for.
